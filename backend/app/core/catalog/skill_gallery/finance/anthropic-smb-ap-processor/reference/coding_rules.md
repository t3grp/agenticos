# Coding proposals

## Evidence order

Use the engagement's documented accounting policy and chart of accounts, actual line substance, PO/job evidence and consistent vendor history. A history of coding one vendor to materials is a useful starting point, not authority to classify this invoice the same way when it buys an asset or a service of a different kind. An explicit reviewer policy takes precedence over a learned pattern.

Propose existing verified account IDs only. If the chart is unreadable, use descriptive suggestions marked unverified and ask for the relevant account mapping. Include the source and reasoning for every uncertain code. Confidence labels are qualitative review aids; do not invent calibrated probability scores.

## Splits and recurring costs

Allocate by supported invoice line, quantity, job or cost-driver evidence. Check that proposed split amounts sum exactly to the original document. When evidence does not establish a split, leave the allocation unresolved; do not spread evenly or assign the entire value to a guessed job merely to complete a table.

Recurring vendors can suggest a code, but recurrence establishes neither current amount nor approval. Compare to prior periods and flag material changes under the engagement's threshold. All coding remains a proposal.

## Categories requiring care

| Situation | Review point |
|---|---|
| Equipment or improvement | Asset/expense treatment and useful life follow the applicable framework and policy. |
| Deposits/prepayments | Distinguish future benefits from current-period expense. |
| Job supplies | Verify job/class/project attribution; do not infer from a vendor name alone. |
| Taxes | Apply the confirmed jurisdiction and invoice evidence; do not invent an input-tax recovery rate. |
| Card or reimbursed expense | Confirm it is already paid and exclude a duplicate payment proposal. |
| Credit note | Tie to the related invoice and verify eligibility before proposing netting. |

Return the invoice/line, proposed account and job, amount/currency, evidence, reasoning and review status. Do not write the code into the ledger.
