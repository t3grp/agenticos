# Currency, jurisdiction, dates and reporting periods

Use the authorized engagement context, not the accounting firm's location or a connector's brand. Reuse confirmed facts and ask only for missing decisions that affect the work.

## Context fields

Record client and legal entity, relevant countries/tax jurisdictions, functional and presentation currencies, transaction currencies, financial year-end, accounting basis/framework, period cut-off and timezone. Read a permitted organization or accounting-period record when available and reconcile it to the engagement. A display name alone does not establish jurisdiction or entity.

## Amounts and FX

Use ISO currency codes such as SGD, GBP or USD beside amounts. Preserve transaction currency and presentation currency separately. Never add currencies or silently convert a payout; show rate, source, date and rounding policy for each material conversion. Spreadsheet formats should carry the currency code, and negative signs must be consistent with the accounting convention.

Rounding/materiality/attention thresholds are engagement decisions. A source example using 0.50, 25 or 200 is not a universal threshold and is not automatically suitable in another currency. Record the chosen threshold and reviewer basis.

## Legal and framework requirements

A legal threshold, tax deadline or filing treatment belongs to a specific regime, entity and effective period. Verify consequential requirements against current primary authorities and preserve citations. Do not reuse a US 1099/SOX/estimated-tax example for another jurisdiction or assume it remains current for the present year. Country alone may be insufficient where cross-border tax obligations apply.

These generic accounting skills do not implement a jurisdiction pack. When the question exceeds the confirmed scope or current evidence, prepare the workpaper and identify the qualified reviewer decision.

## Dates and horizons

Resolve “this quarter,” “year to date” and “last financial year” against the confirmed fiscal calendar; ask when ambiguity changes the output. Use explicit start/end dates and inclusion conventions. Keep invoice, due, service, posting, payment and retrieval dates distinct. Filenames use YYYY-MM-DD. Narrative dates should be unambiguous and carry a timezone where time of day matters.

Illustrative source figures may remain in their example currency. They must never become client data or default regulatory assumptions.
